What is Digital Literacy?

Digital literacy refers to the ability to use digital technologies effectively, responsibly, and safely. A digitally literate person can locate information online, communicate using digital tools, create digital content, and evaluate information critically.

Digital literacy is important because technology is used in education, business, healthcare, entertainment, communication, banking, tourism, and government services. In the Caribbean, it has become increasingly vital as schools, businesses, and public services rely more heavily on technology.

A person who lacks digital literacy may struggle to complete schoolwork, apply for jobs, communicate professionally, or recognise online dangers such as scams and misinformation.

Why Digital Literacy is Important

  • Communicate effectively
  • Access information quickly
  • Complete school and work tasks efficiently
  • Stay safe online
  • Participate in modern society
  • Create and share content responsibly

Characteristics of a Digitally Literate Person

  • Uses technology confidently
  • Understands online safety
  • Respects others online
  • Uses productivity tools effectively
  • Evaluates online information carefully
  • Understands ethical behaviour online

🔑 Key Terms

Digital LiteracyThe ability to use digital tools effectively and responsibly
Digital TechnologyElectronic tools and systems used to create, store, and share information
Digital MediaContent created and shared digitally such as videos, images, podcasts, and websites

What is Digital Technology?

Digital technology includes electronic devices and systems that process information using binary code. Examples include smartphones, laptops, tablets, smart TVs, and gaming consoles.

Examples of Digital Technology

  • Computers
  • Smartphones
  • Tablets
  • Smart watches
  • Interactive whiteboards
  • Digital cameras
  • Wireless routers

Digital Media

Digital media refers to any media content stored or transmitted digitally, including videos, images, podcasts, music, websites, social media posts, and online advertisements.

Wireless Technology

Wireless devices connect to networks without physical cables — for example smartphones, Bluetooth speakers, wireless headphones, tablets, and smart TVs.

✅ Advantages
  • Portability
  • Convenience
  • Mobility
  • Faster communication
❌ Disadvantages
  • Security risks
  • Dependence on internet
  • Battery limitations

Digital Citizenship and Online Responsibility
Digital footprints, netiquette, responsible behaviour
›

What is Digital Citizenship?

Digital citizenship refers to responsible, safe, and ethical behaviour when using digital technologies.

Characteristics of a Good Digital Citizen

  • Respect others online
  • Protect personal information
  • Communicate politely
  • Follow laws and rules
  • Think critically about information

Digital Footprint

A digital footprint is the trail of information left online through activities such as posting, commenting, browsing, searching, and sharing content. Even deleted posts may remain accessible through screenshots or archives.

Employers and schools may review a person's online presence before offering opportunities, so students should think carefully before posting online.

Active Digital Footprint

Intentionally shared online

  • Social media posts
  • Comments
  • Uploaded videos
Passive Digital Footprint

Collected without direct user input

  • Cookies
  • Browsing history
  • Location tracking

Managing Your Digital Footprint

  • Think before posting
  • Use strong privacy settings
  • Avoid oversharing personal information
  • Use respectful language online

Netiquette

Netiquette refers to proper online behaviour. Examples: using respectful language, avoiding typing in ALL CAPS, responding politely, respecting others' opinions.

🔑 Key Terms

Digital CitizenshipResponsible online behaviour
Digital FootprintInformation left online by a user

Intellectual Property and Copyright

Intellectual Property Rights

Intellectual property rights protect creations of the mind such as music, books, inventions, and artwork.

Copyright

Copyright is legal protection given to creators of original work. Copyrighted materials include songs, movies, books, photographs, and software.

Plagiarism

Plagiarism occurs when someone uses another person's work without giving credit. Examples: copying from a website without citing the source, submitting another person's work as your own.

How to Avoid Plagiarism

  • Cite sources properly
  • Use quotation marks for direct quotes
  • Paraphrase information correctly

Piracy

Piracy is the illegal copying or distribution of copyrighted material. Examples include downloading movies illegally, using cracked software, and sharing copyrighted music illegally.

🔑 Key Terms

CopyrightLegal protection for original work
PlagiarismUsing another's work without credit
PiracyIllegal copying of digital content

Cyber Safety and Online Threats
Cyberbullying, phishing, identity theft, staying safe
›

Cyberbullying

Cyberbullying is bullying that takes place online. Examples: sending hurtful messages, sharing embarrassing photos, spreading rumours online.

Effects of Cyberbullying

  • Anxiety
  • Depression
  • Low self-esteem
  • Social isolation

Phishing

Phishing is a scam where criminals attempt to trick people into revealing personal information such as passwords, banking details, or account information. Attacks often appear as emails, texts, or fake websites pretending to be legitimate organisations.

Signs of Phishing

  • Suspicious links
  • Requests for passwords
  • Poor spelling and grammar
  • Urgent messages

Identity Theft

Identity theft occurs when someone steals another person's personal information to use fraudulently.

Cybercrime

Cybercrime refers to crimes committed using digital technology. Examples: hacking, online fraud, identity theft, phishing scams.

Staying Safe Online

  • Use strong passwords
  • Avoid suspicious links
  • Enable two-factor authentication
  • Keep software updated
  • Never share private information publicly

🔑 Key Terms

CyberbullyingBullying using digital technology
PhishingAttempt to steal information online
Identity TheftStealing someone's personal information
CybercrimeCrimes committed using digital technology

What is Virtual Reality?

Virtual reality (VR) is technology that creates a simulated digital environment. Users can experience and interact with this environment using special headsets and controllers.

Uses of Virtual Reality

  • Gaming
  • Education
  • Healthcare training (e.g. surgery simulations)
  • Tourism
  • Architecture
  • Therapy and sports training
✅ Advantages
  • Interactive learning
  • Realistic simulations
  • Enhanced engagement
❌ Disadvantages
  • Expensive equipment
  • Motion sickness
  • Limited accessibility

Other Emerging Technologies

  • Artificial Intelligence (AI)
  • Augmented Reality (AR)
  • Robotics
  • Smart devices
